## 2. Issues found while testing

### 2.1 Triple-click selection is rejected (bug)

Steps: open a book and triple-click a paragraph.

Observed: the toolbar says "Select text within one paragraph for passage tools." The Inspector still shows "Selected passage: and", a leftover from the double-click part of the triple-click.

Cause: browsers end a triple-click range at offset 0 of the *next* paragraph. `captureSelection` in [App.tsx:764](../src/App.tsx) sees two paragraphs and returns, and the old `selection` state is not cleared.

Fix:
- If `range.endOffset === 0` and the end container is the start of the next `[data-paragraph]`, clamp the range end to the end of the start paragraph.
- On any rejected selection, call `setSelection(null)` so the inspector never shows stale text.
- Consider letting multi-paragraph selections use "Read selection" and "Copy". Only annotations need single-paragraph ranges.

### 2.2 Other observations

- **Contents list** shows "I, II, III" without the subtitle. The chapter subtitle is a separate heading paragraph. Merge it into the TOC label when a heading is a bare numeral.
- **Pagination**: pages are text-based and stable (an earlier draft of this review wrongly said the count changed with the inspector). A count in the thousands still gives little orientation, so the player now shows the chapter and the time left in it.
- **Player bar** shows "Passage playback 0:00 / 0:00" when idle. Show the chapter name and time left instead. When idle and a book is open, the Play button should say what it will do, for example a tooltip reading "Read from Chapter III, paragraph 4".
- **Typography**: no font family, margins, justification or hyphenation controls. WebView2 supports `hyphens: auto` for English.
- **Settings and Appearance duplicate each other**: the same controls appear in Settings and in the Appearance tab. That is fine, but reader-specific ones (font, size, spacing) should open from an "Aa" button in the reader header, like Kindle and Apple Books. That is where users look first.
- **Uncommitted work**: about 30 modified or new files, including the installer, reading pages and the speech worker. Commit before the next large change.
- **Assets**: the four season PNGs are 2.0 to 2.6 MB each. With eight day/night scenes, convert to WebP or AVIF at quality 80 to 85. Expect roughly 300 to 500 KB each with no visible loss on a blurred, dimmed background.

## 3. What the market says (condensed)

Reddit could not be fetched. Sources are GitHub issue trackers (Readest, Thorium, Foliate, KOReader, abogen), Hacker News, app-store aggregators, AppleVis and review sites. Competitor-owned blogs are weaker evidence.

**Closest competitors.** Readest is also Tauri-based and cross-platform, and its issue tracker is close to a free roadmap. Calibre's viewer now ships local Piper (7.18) and Kokoro (9.16) voices, so "offline neural voices on Windows" alone is no longer unique. Origami's distinct angle is the combination: spoken-version editing, higher-quality local voices, voice catalog, and atmosphere.

### Loved

1. Natural voices. Robotic voices are the number-one reason people quit read-aloud.
2. Read-along highlighting. Dyslexic and ADHD readers describe it as what makes reading possible at all.
3. Offline, private, no account. Users call cloud TTS that sends every paragraph away "creepy" (Readest #258). Edge Natural voices also disappeared for iOS users in Sept 2026.
4. Pronunciation and skip rules (Balabolka, Voice Dream, @Voice).
5. One-time or free pricing. Speechify billing and the Voice Dream subscription switch caused major backlash.
6. Sleep timer, speed control without pitch change, media keys.
7. Ambient sound under narration (Speechify). This connects directly to Origami's scenes.

### Complained about

1. Subscriptions and surprise billing.
2. Playback stalls, skips, starting in the wrong place, and losing position.
3. Junk read aloud: page numbers, footnote markers, citations, URLs. The Thorium lesson is that silent skipping confuses people, so skipping must be visible.
4. Pauses between sentences that are too short or too long.
5. Mispronunciations that cannot be fixed.
6. Highlight drift at higher speeds.
7. Player redesigns that hide time-left or cover text.
8. Local TTS setup friction: CUDA not detected, failed Windows installs, TTS hidden until configured.
9. Slow import of big libraries, and no folder import.

### Requested or missing

1. Engine choice, including local engines.
2. Pronunciation dictionary.
3. Skip-rule checklist.
4. Word-level highlight with a sentence-level toggle.
5. Audio export per chapter (MP3/M4B).
6. Character voices.
7. Shortcuts and media keys.
8. Sync and backup without lock-in (WebDAV or a folder).
9. PDF.
10. Settings applied to all books.
11. Dictionary lookup that pauses TTS automatically.
12. Sentence-by-sentence step mode.
13. Sleep timer.

### Accessibility

- **Screen readers.** Thorium is the Windows reference: tested with NVDA, JAWS and Narrator, with remappable shortcuts. Its known pitfalls are Windows High Contrast and controls disappearing at 150 to 200 % display scaling.
- **Dyslexia.** Spacing and slower speech (0.8 to 0.9x) have evidence. OpenDyslexic has none, but some users like it, so offer it as an option.
- **ADHD.** Faster speech, line focus and dimming help.
- **Bionic reading.** Two studies show no benefit, so it is low priority.
- **Motion.** Animated backgrounds need an off switch and must keep text contrast. Origami already does this well.

Sources: see the full source list in section 8.

## 4. Recommendations by area

### 4.1 First run and "just press Play"

- The first-run idea in IDEAS.md is good. Keep it to three screens: pick a scene and day/night, pick a voice (with a 5 s preview each), then open a starter book. Do not ask about motion, opacity or texture there. Defaults are fine and the settings remain.
- If no speech engine is configured, Play should still be visible. Clicking it should open a small "Set up voices" panel, never a dead button (the Foliate lesson). As a fallback, offer Windows OneCore/SAPI voices via `speechSynthesis` so Play always produces sound. Label them clearly as "System voice".
- Show model load progress in the player ("Loading Maeve · 4 s") rather than a silent wait.

### 4.2 Library

- Drag and drop files or folders onto the window, plus Import folder (recursive). This is a top Readest and Foliate request.
- A "Continue reading" card exists. Add a **Continue listening** action that resumes audio from the last spoken sentence in one click.
- Show estimated listening time per book (words / (155 wpm x speed)) next to the word count. People choose audiobooks by length.
- Duplicate detection on import (title + author + size hash) with Keep both / Replace.
- List view: sortable columns (title, author, progress, added, length).

### 4.3 Reader

- An "Aa" quick panel in the reader header with font, size, spacing, surface, and the day/night toggle. Settings stays the full version.
- Font family, letter and word spacing, margins, justify on/off, hyphenation.
- Chapter titles in Contents, a current-chapter marker that follows narration, and collapsible parts for long books.
- **Find in book** results list with surrounding context and a match count. M5 covers search; make sure it shows context.
- Optional line focus or reading ruler: dims everything except the current line or paragraph. It pairs naturally with narration.
- Dictionary lookup on double-click with an offline dictionary (Wiktionary dump or WordNet), pausing TTS while open. This could come after v1.
- Mouse wheel turns pages in page mode, as an option.

### 4.4 Listening and player

- **Granularity**: previous/next should skip by sentence. Hold the button, or use Shift+arrow, to skip by paragraph. Double-click a paragraph while playing to jump there.
- **Highlight**: start with the current sentence highlighted inside the current passage (cheap: you already split sentences), then word-level later (X2). Always provide a toggle between word, sentence, paragraph and off.
- **Pauses**: sliders for the gap after a sentence, after a paragraph and after a heading. Default to about 250, 600 and 1000 ms.
- **Read-ahead**: one passage of read-ahead can leave gaps with a heavy model. Make read-ahead depth adaptive: measure the generation real-time factor, keep N passages buffered so the buffer covers at least 20 s, and show a small buffer indicator.
- **Error recovery**: if a passage fails, retry once, then skip it and mark it in the margin with "Not read, retry". Never stop the session (the top Speechify and Readest complaint).
- **Sleep timer** with "end of chapter" and a fade-out. Add "Stop at end of chapter" as a one-click option.
- **Mini player**: when the window is narrow or minimised, a compact always-on-top player (Tauri window) with Play, Skip and the current sentence (M16).
- **Speed**: 0.5 to 3.0 in 0.05 steps, with pitch preserved (`preservesPitch` defaults on in Chromium; keep it explicitly).
- **Ambient sound (NEW, fits your identity)**: optional low-volume lake, rain, fire or night-crickets loop per scene, ducked automatically under narration. Speechify users single this out as a delight, and it ties the scenes to listening.

### 4.5 Speech text quality

- Promote "Skip links, markup and decorative symbols" into a visible **Skip rules** checklist:
  - footnote markers and superscripts
  - bracketed citations [12] and (Smith 2020)
  - URLs
  - page numbers and running heads (for PDF later)
  - text in tables
- Show skipped spans faintly in the reader while narrating, so skipping is never mysterious.
- **Pronunciation dictionary** with scopes (this book, this voice, everywhere), whole-word and case options, a test button, and import/export as CSV. Spoken versions remain the passage-level tool. The dictionary handles repeated names.
- Expand abbreviations by rule (Mr., Dr., St., No., vol.) and numbers, dates and currency. Many local models handle these badly.
- Dialogue awareness, as a lighter version of Studio: a slight pause before and after quotes, optionally a second voice for quoted speech. This comes before full character attribution (X3).

### 4.6 Appearance

- Day/night per scene (section 5).
- **Contrast guard**: compute the contrast of the reading text against the effective surface (brightness slider x opacity x scene) and warn or clamp below WCAG 4.5:1. Brightness at 50 % on Paper with dark text can fail.
- Respect Windows High Contrast (`forced-colors: active`) by disabling scenes and using system colors. The High Contrast themes exist, but the OS mode should trigger them.
- UI scale (90 to 130 %). Test at Windows display scaling of 150 % and 200 %, where Thorium hides controls.

### 4.7 Settings organization

- Left section nav: Reading, Listening, Voices, Appearance, Library, Covers & art, Storage, Shortcuts, About.
- A search box filters settings by label.
- "Reset this section" per section.
- An "Advanced" disclosure for model preload, randomize voice, cover engine and NSFW. These are power features that new users do not need to read on the first scroll.

### 4.8 Data safety and trust

- One-click **Backup library** (SQLite + annotations + spoken edits) to a zip, and Restore. Before sync, a "backup to a folder you choose" (OneDrive or Dropbox folder) covers most of the sync demand with almost no work.
- Annotation export to Markdown in Obsidian-friendly format and to CSV. Readwise-style highlight export is a frequent ask.
- Pricing message: since the release is pay-what-you-want, say plainly in About and on the site, "No account, no subscription, works offline." The research shows this is a reason people switch.

## 5. Day and night scenes

### 5.1 Model

Replace the flat scene list with **season x time of day**:

```ts
type Season = "spring" | "summer" | "autumn" | "winter";
type TimeOfDay = "day" | "night";
type SceneId = `${Season}-${TimeOfDay}`; // "spring-day", "spring-night", ...
```

- `prefs.scene` becomes `{ season, time }`, or keep the string ids above. Migrate `lake` to `spring-night` (the moonlit lake already has blossoms and a moon). Map `spring` to `spring-day`, `summer` to `summer-night` (it is already blue hour), `autumn` to `autumn-night` (dusk with lit cabin) and `winter` to `winter-day`. That leaves four new images to make: summer day, autumn day, winter night and possibly a true night version of summer if blue hour is kept as day. Keep "None" and "Paper hills" outside the grid.
- `scenePreferences` is keyed per variant, so day and night each remember their own palette, surface, opacity and effects. Suggested defaults:

| Variant | Palette | Surface | Opacity | Effects |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Spring day | Sage Morning | Paper | 70 % | petals, mist, sky clouds, ripples |
| Spring night | Plum Nocturne | Night | 50 % | petals, mist, fog, ripples, cabin lights, moon cloud |
| Summer day | Forest Ink | Paper | 70 % | sky clouds, fish ripples, ripples |
| Summer night | Burgundy Velvet | Sepia or Night | 60 % | fireflies, fish ripples, fog, cabin lights, moon cloud |
| Autumn day | Warm Parchment | Sepia | 70 % | leaves, mist, sky clouds, ripples |
| Autumn night | Copper Hearth | Sepia | 65 % | leaves, mist, fog, cabin lights |
| Winter day | Paper White | Paper | 85 % | snow, mist, sky clouds, ripples |
| Winter night | Graphite or Moonlit Lake | Night | 55 % | snow, fog, cabin lights, moon cloud |

- Effects should be filtered by time: `fireflies`, `cabinLights` and `moonCloud` are night only, and `skyClouds` is day only. Define `effects` per variant in `backgrounds.ts` and the panel automatically shows the right checkboxes.

### 5.2 Toggle behavior

- A Sun/Moon button in the header next to the palette button, also in the "Aa" panel. Shortcut: Ctrl+Shift+L.
- Toggling keeps the season and swaps the variant, with a 1.2 s crossfade between the two images. Effects fade in and out. Respect reduced motion by cutting instantly.
- **Time selection** becomes a second dropdown next to Background selection:
  - Manual (Sun/Moon button)
  - Follow Windows light/dark mode (`prefers-color-scheme`)
  - Follow local time: day 07:00 to 19:00 by default, editable. Optionally use sunrise/sunset computed offline from a city or latitude typed by the user. No network or geolocation is needed.
- Combined with "Current season" mode, the app becomes fully automatic: autumn evening in October shows autumn night.
- If the user picks a palette manually while in a variant, save it to that variant only, as now.

### 5.3 Producing the art

- Reuse the edit-the-same-composition prompts in docs/SEASONAL-PROMPTS.md. For each night variant, add: "Night version of this exact image. Moon at 93.6 % across and 24.2 % down, cabin window warmly lit at its original location with a soft reflection, starfield faint, foliage colors desaturated by moonlight. No baked-in particles." For day variants, use "unlit window, no moon, light from upper right".
- Generate night from the matching day image, not from a different season, so the foliage matches exactly. Check alignment by overlaying at 50 % opacity. The cabin window and moon must line up because the coded cabin-light and moon-cloud layers use fixed coordinates.
- Ship as WebP or AVIF at 2560 x 1440. Preload only the active variant and its twin (for an instant toggle), and lazy-load the rest.
- Update the thumbnail grid to four season tiles, each split diagonally day/night, with the current half outlined. This keeps the picker at four choices instead of eight.

## 6. Feature-rich without clutter: principles to keep

1. **One obvious action per screen.** Library: Continue. Reader: Play. Everything else lives one level down.
2. **Context first, settings second.** Put controls where they are used (the "Aa" panel, right-click on a word, the speed menu in the player), and keep full Settings for depth.
3. **Visible defaults with overrides.** Per-book overrides with "Apply to all books" and "Reset to default" buttons.
4. **Never silent.** Skipped text, failed passages, model loading and long jobs always show state.
5. **Never lose the place.** Resume at the exact sentence. Survive crashes (save position every sentence, not on a timer only).
6. **Do not redesign under people's feet.** Once the beta has testers, ship layout changes behind a toggle for one release.
